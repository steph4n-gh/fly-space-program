# Shared luminance inputs limit chemical predictions

Shared high/low inputs impose an exact representational limit. For a fixed cell and flash polarity, let F(theta; u, x0, H) be the predicted observed curve, with parameters theta, stimulus u, initial state x0, and observation operator H. The archived fit uses the same u, x0, H and theta for both condition labels. It computes one dark and one bright prediction, then reuses them for the four flash targets (tested-fit-source.py, lines 57–78). Thus F_high(theta)=F_low(theta) for every admissible theta.

For arbitrary common control and treatment vectors theta0 and theta1, Delta_high=F_high(theta1)-F_high(theta0)=F_low(theta1)-F_low(theta0)=Delta_low. Every common linear window average has the same equality. This covers changes to w or any other condition-independent parameters, including finite nonlinear changes. No fit or simulation is needed. Changing shared parameters cannot reproduce opposite high/low change signs while these input, initialization and observation assumptions remain fixed.

The completed diagnostic contains these opposite raw mean changes (CDM minus control, fractional ΔF/F):

| Cell and bright-flash window | highLum | lowLum |
|---|---:|---:|
| L1, late (0.05–0.25 s) | +0.001768783078 | −0.000274800628 |
| L2, early (0–0.05 s) | +0.003035644679 | −0.002643223541 |

All four values exceed the diagnostic's 1e−9 numerical sign tolerance in magnitude. That tolerance establishes numerical classification only. Controls contain exported means without individual response arrays; animal pairing and independent sampling units are unverified. These opposite sample-mean signs therefore do not establish a statistically supported biological interaction. Fixed windows also differ from the paper's phase analysis. The conclusion is inability to reproduce these reported point estimates, not rejection of octopamine biology. Stochastic observations could show opposite sample means around a shared expected change.

At least one sign disagreement is unavoidable in each of the two high/low pairs above. Consequently 14/16 is an upper bound on agreement with all reported primary-window signs under the shared-context assumptions, not an attainable score claim. The observed 12/16 local-w result mixes these structural conflicts with additional parameter-dependent mismatches; larger shared changes cannot resolve the structural two.
